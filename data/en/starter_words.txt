# Starter words — the English a learner has before this begins.
#
# Every line here counts as ALREADY KNOWN when the roadmap decides which
# sentences are i+1, beside function_words.txt. They are the commonest
# content words of English: the ones a sentence is built around, which a
# learner preparing for an exam like YDS has known for years.
#
# Why there is a list at all: with only the function words known, almost
# every sentence holds two or three words to learn, and a plan aimed at an
# academic word list has nowhere to begin. Measured on 3,521 conversational
# English subtitle lines, the function words alone leave 53 readable and 424
# one word away; with this list, 1,260 readable and 1,356 one word away --
# about where the one-word-away count peaks, so it is the size that gives the
# walk the most to choose from without assuming rarer words nobody checked.
#
# HOW TO REVIEW
#   Each reader works from a copy of this file, their known_words.txt, made
#   when they are added. Strike what they do not know there -- put a  in
#   front of the line, or answer no in the quiz, which does the same. This
#   file is the language's, shared by every English reader, and stays as is.
#
# SOURCE
#   The first 1,000 lemmas of the New General Service List 1.2, in its
#   frequency order (SFI rank 1 to 1,143), leaving out what the function
#   words already cover. New General Service List by Browne, C., Culligan,
#   B., and Phillips, J., https://www.newgeneralservicelist.com, licensed
#   under Creative Commons Attribution-ShareAlike 4.0 International
#   (https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by-sa/4.0/). This file is an
#   excerpt of it and is shared under the same licence.
