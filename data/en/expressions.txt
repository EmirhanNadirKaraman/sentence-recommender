# English expressions: units of their own, found by their words.
#
# One a line, `canonical<TAB>pattern`. The canonical is the unit's key: what
# the card is titled and the study list names. Being in this file is what
# registers it -- see `Application.registered_patterns`. A sentence that says
# an expression yields it *instead of* the words it is made of: `She gave up`
# is `she` and `give up`, not `give` and `up` as well, because knowing `give`
# is not knowing `give up` and reading the sentence needs only the second.
#
# The pattern says how it is found (`corpus.english.Expression`):
#
#     give :: up            a verb, and words hanging from it anywhere in its
#                           clause: `gave up`, `gave it up`, `has given up`.
#                           A word with `sth` or `sb` after it leads a phrase
#                           of its own (`depend :: on sth` -- `depends on the
#                           weather`); one without must not, which is what
#                           keeps `went to London on Monday` from being `go on`
#     in spite of           words in a row, each matched by its form or its
#                           lemma; `[x]` may be absent, `*` is any one word
#                           or none, `a/b` is either
#
# With no pattern, the canonical's own words are looked for in order with up
# to two others between them, and `sb`, `sth`, `one's` stand for a phrase.
#
# Only what is a word of its own belongs here. Every line is a unit a reader
# has to learn, and a sentence saying one that is not on their list is one
# they cannot count as readable under strict counting until they do.
#
# Changing this file changes how the English corpus is analysed: rebuild it
# (`build-corpus subtitle --reader NAME`) after editing.
