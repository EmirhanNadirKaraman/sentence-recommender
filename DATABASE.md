One Postgres database, `DB_NAME`, in schemas. `public` holds the
catalogue and the corpus -- what German exists, and what was made of
it -- and `en` the same tables for English. `accounts` holds who the
readers are, and `reader` and each `reader_<name>` what one reader has
decided.

## Schema `reader` — what one reader does (42 tables)

One schema per reader, all alike: `reader` for the installation's own,
whose this is, and `reader_<name>` for each reader added since. What
the reader has done, and what has been derived or paid for on their
behalf. Roadmaps and scores are derived and rebuildable. The
judgements are not: `known_units`, `checked_units`, `snoozed_units`, `hidden_sentences`, `corrected_sentences`, `video_attempts` hold decisions
nothing can regenerate. A dump of the corpus to hand to someone else
leaves these schemas out, and `accounts` with them (README, "Moving
the corpus").

## Schema `public` — the German catalogue (12 tables)

This project's own database, under `alembic` at revision `b31e7c0d9a42`. Every
read and every write goes here; `DB_NAME` names it.

It began as a copy of language-app's `german_vocabulary`, made by
`sync-catalogue --from german_vocabulary`. That was a migration, not a
link: ingestion writes here now, so re-copying would destroy work, and
the command refuses when it would.

Tables marked *write-only* are filled by language-app's scraper during
ingestion and read by nothing in this project.

## Schema `public` — the analysed German corpus (3 tables + 1 view)

What the matcher made of the catalogue: one row per sentence worth
reading, and one per learning unit in it. `build` names both the source
and how it was assembled, so two ways of cutting the same subtitles can
coexist; `build_meta` records which rules produced each.

These were SQLite until the i+1 subtraction — 3.48M set operations per
load — was measured at 2.18s in SQL against 10.04s in Python.
